The Gallery

the art, and how to keep a piece of it

Every piece started somewhere ordinary — a pile of books on a cold Paris morning, the corner of a room, an afternoon that didn’t seem like much at the time. Nicolas photographed it, tore the print up on purpose, and layered it back together until it turned into somewhere you could sit inside for a minute.

The Gallery is where those pieces get to be big instead of pocket-sized: the art from the mantra deck hung next to the pieces that never made it onto a card, with enough room around each one that you can actually stand there and look at it.

“Close enough to see the paper fibers, and the bit of glue that never dried flat.”

further down the road

Taking a piece home

I’ve always thought this work belongs on a wall rather than behind glass on a phone, so the plan is for the pieces you fall for to become yours to keep — printed properly, on stock heavy enough that you can still see the tear and the little shadow it throws.

There will be a framed version too, put together so it’s ready to hang the moment it’s in your hands, with the frame chosen to stay quiet and let the collage do the talking. None of that exists yet — I’m still testing papers and getting the colours to sit the way they do in the original — so the only thing I can offer you today is a promise that when the first pieces are finished, the hive hears about it before anyone else.

Make It Yours

your photo, my hands

For years I’ve been doing this to my own photographs — tearing them apart, layering them, turning an ordinary afternoon into somewhere you’d actually want to live for a minute.

Make It Yours is that, for your photo. Send the one you can’t stop looking at — the dog, the kitchen table, your grandmother’s hands — and I’ll rebuild it in the same torn-paper world the cards live in. Illustrated. Animated. A little alive.

“Same hands, same scissors. Different afternoon.”

It isn’t open yet. I’m still working out how to do it properly — one at a time, slowly, without turning it into a factory.

Put me in the first handful

How it’ll go, roughly:

You pick a photo that matters. We talk about what you love in it.

I tear it up on purpose, and put it back together the long way.

You get your afternoon back, as a scrapbook world.

— no forms to fill in yet, just a list to be on

The Marketplace

a corner for the crew

not a storefront — a hub

I don’t make things alone

The Marketplace is for two kinds of people: the ones who helped build this — a photographer I’ve worked with for years, and others whose hands are in it — and the ones from my community whose work I want more people to see.

A small corner, chosen by hand, one person at a time, where nobody bought their way in. Nothing is set up yet, and nobody has been volunteered for anything — I’d rather ask properly first.

Ten empty cells in the comb. Every one of them is a seat waiting for a person — no names up yet, because nobody has said yes yet.

The captain & the crew

“I am the captain of the boat, but I am no one without the crew — and the crew members are sacred to me.” Community isn’t a section of this site. It’s the operating principle.

Playfulness. Childlike excitement. Love. Empowerment. Community.

The Library

a shelf that grows

mostly empty, on purpose

The things worth reading

Some of To Bee Honest came out of my own head. A lot of it came out of other people’s — books, essays, scraps of writing I kept coming back to until they quietly became part of how I see things.

“Nothing to finish. Nothing due back.”

The Library is where I’ll keep those, next to writing of my own that doesn’t belong on a card. A shelf that grows slowly, in no particular order. It’s empty for now — I’m still deciding what earns a spot on it.
